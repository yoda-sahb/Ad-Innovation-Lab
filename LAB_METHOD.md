# The Ad Innovation Lab Method

> **Personal, independent, company-agnostic R&D.** This method is for my own public idea exploration. It is not an employer innovation process, client framework, or representation of any company's roadmap.

The lab has two layers:

1. **Idea Radar** — loose, speculative, allowed to be weird.
2. **Deep Dives** — ideas that have earned enough structure to deserve a mechanism, proof design, and kill condition.

The goal is not to force every thought into a business case. The goal is to know which thoughts are still just interesting and which ones are ready to be challenged seriously.

## Start with the problem that survives the roadmap

A deep-dive problem usually has several properties:

- it survives multiple product cycles;
- the same failure keeps returning under different names;
- solving it could create reusable capability rather than a one-off feature;
- the obvious answers are structurally incomplete; and
- the economics or decision quality could be material enough to justify changing the system.

## The sequence

### 1. Possibility
State the structural problem or opportunity in one sentence.

Do not start with a solution name.

### 2. System boundary
Define what must change and what deliberately stays untouched.

This is where many wild ideas become much smaller and more useful.

### 3. Mechanism
Identify the smallest causal change that could alter the outcome.

The mechanism should make clear:

- who owns the decision;
- what information is required;
- what rights and constraints remain intact;
- what changes in the system; and
- what explicitly does not change.

### 4. Proof
Define the smallest credible experiment.

At minimum:

- baseline;
- treatment;
- primary metric;
- guardrails;
- evidence / causality method;
- time window; and
- operational constraints.

### 5. Kill test
Write the result that causes the idea to stop, narrow, or return to the radar.

If the idea cannot lose, it is not yet research.

### 6. Prototype
Build only enough artifact to answer a real uncertainty.

A prototype can test feasibility, semantics, workflow, latency, architecture, or participant behavior. It cannot by itself prove causal business value.

### 7. Evidence
Separate:

- documented external evidence;
- strategic interpretation;
- working hypothesis;
- prototype behavior; and
- measured causal result.

Never collapse these into “proven.”

### 8. Decision
The outcome can be:

- **BUILD / TEST** — the mechanism is worth a stronger experiment;
- **NARROW** — part of the thesis works but the architecture is larger than necessary;
- **HOLD** — interesting, but a prerequisite or market condition is missing;
- **KILL** — the current system is good enough, the mechanism failed, or the economics do not justify the change; or
- **RADAR** — still worth thinking about, but not mature enough for serious architecture.

## Craziness is a dependency score

The Idea Radar uses “craziness” deliberately, but it has a concrete meaning:

`1/5` mostly internal product/decisioning change.  
`2/5` new reusable product or control layer.  
`3/5` coordination across multiple systems or companies.  
`4/5` new portable market primitive or contract.  
`5/5` changes what the market clears, owns, settles, or treats as the basic economic unit.

A `5/5` idea is not better than a `1/5` idea. Usually it is less likely to survive.

## Probability is a prior, not a prediction

The radar's five-year percentages are deliberately rough research priors. They mean how likely I currently think some useful product, system, or primitive in that direction becomes materially relevant within roughly five years.

They are there to expose my assumptions, not to manufacture precision.

## Anti-patterns

### Architecture-first innovation
A large architecture is designed before anyone proves the root problem cannot be solved inside the current system.

### Demo theater
A prototype is treated as evidence that customers, economics, or market structure will behave as assumed.

### AI wallpaper
A model or agent is inserted into an existing workflow without identifying a new system-level advantage.

### Standard before value
The ecosystem is asked to coordinate before one bounded decision demonstrates enough value to justify coordination.

### Incentive fantasy
The idea works only if another actor voluntarily stops spending, surrenders economics, gives up control, or behaves against its incentives.

### Seriousness inflation
A thought gets a polished name and suddenly gets treated like a product thesis.

That is exactly why the radar exists.

## Public boundary

Everything here is independent personal research. Company examples, when used, are market context only. The lab intentionally excludes confidential implementation details, restricted prior-employer material, and claims that these concepts are deployed, adopted, patented, or endorsed by any company.

**Wild ideas are cheap. Useful falsification is the work.**

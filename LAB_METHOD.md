# The Ad Innovation Lab Method

> **Personal, independent, company-agnostic R&D.** This public document explains the method, not the private research notebook or implementation details of individual concepts.

The lab is designed for one recurring problem: a compelling idea can become architecture long before the underlying mechanism has earned it.

The operating sequence is:

`PROBLEM → BOUNDARY → MECHANISM → PROOF → KILL TEST → BUILD / HOLD / KILL`

## 1. Start with a problem that survives the roadmap

A serious research problem usually has several properties:

- it can survive multiple product cycles;
- the same failure returns under different names;
- solving it could create reusable capability rather than a one-off feature;
- the obvious answers appear structurally incomplete; and
- the economics or decision quality could be material enough to justify changing the system.

## 2. Define the system boundary

Ask what actually has to change and what should deliberately stay untouched.

A good boundary often makes an ambitious idea smaller, cheaper, and more testable.

## 3. Find the mechanism

Identify the smallest causal change that could alter the outcome.

The private research version may include architecture, decision rights, data requirements, constraints, and implementation details. Those mechanics are not automatically part of the public version.

## 4. Design proof, not demo theater

A serious test needs a baseline, treatment, primary metric, guardrails, causality/evidence method, time window, and operating constraints.

A prototype can answer feasibility questions. It does not by itself prove customer value or causal business impact.

## 5. Write the kill test

State the result that causes the idea to stop, narrow, or return to research.

If an idea cannot lose, it is not yet research.

## 6. Separate evidence classes

Keep distinct:

- documented external evidence;
- strategic interpretation;
- working hypothesis;
- prototype behavior; and
- measured causal result.

Never collapse them into “proven.”

## 7. Decide

A research line can end in:

- **BUILD / TEST** — worth a stronger experiment;
- **NARROW** — part of the thesis survives but the proposed system is larger than necessary;
- **HOLD** — a prerequisite or market condition is missing;
- **KILL** — the mechanism failed or the existing system is good enough; or
- **RESEARCH** — still interesting, but not mature enough for architecture.

## Behavioral reality

A strategy also has to survive the humans and institutions inside the system.

If the thesis only works because another actor voluntarily stops spending, surrenders economics, gives up control, or lets resources disappear, the model is incomplete. Saved or displaced resources normally go somewhere.

## Publication boundary

The lab is **private by default**.

A public derivative may show:

- the structural problem;
- a high-level thesis;
- why the question matters; and
- the broad condition that would make the thesis stronger or weaker.

The following stay private unless explicitly cleared:

- detailed mechanisms and architectures;
- algorithms, schemas, state models, and decision logic;
- detailed experiment designs and thresholds;
- prototypes exposing the mechanism;
- prior-art and patent/claim analysis;
- company-specific overlays and assumptions; and
- unpublished concept inventory.

The objective is to make the thinking inspectable without making the invention reproducible.

**Wild ideas are cheap. Useful falsification is the work.**

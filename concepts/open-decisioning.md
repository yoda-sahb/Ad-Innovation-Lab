# OpenDecisioning

## Let specialized intelligence contribute without forcing everyone into one brain

**Status:** `WORKING R&D`  
**Type:** bounded open-market decision architecture

### Possibility

Open advertising systems already contain useful intelligence in many places:

- publishers;
- advertisers;
- commerce and retail systems;
- measurement providers;
- DSPs and SSPs;
- identity / data systems; and
- specialist models.

The problem is not simply moving more data into one platform.

The harder question is whether independently owned intelligence can improve a shared economic decision **without centralizing the raw data, model, or decision rights.**

### Mechanism

Separate the roles that are often collapsed inside vertically integrated systems:

- **rights holder** — owns the permissible action space;
- **intelligence provider** — contributes a bounded conclusion;
- **coordinator** — determines which eligible intelligence is relevant;
- **executor** — performs the market action; and
- **evidence provider** — binds outcomes back to the decision.

A specialist should be able to contribute only the decision-relevant output, with enough provenance, confidence, scope, freshness, permissions, and constraints to use it safely.

The system should record what intelligence was used, what was rejected, and whether it caused incremental value.

### First proof

Do not start with a universal protocol.

Start with one bounded economic decision and one independently owned specialist input.

A minimal experiment compares:

1. baseline decisioning;
2. specialist A alone;
3. specialist B alone; and
4. compatible composed intelligence.

The objective is causal contribution, not model novelty.

### What makes this different from model hosting

Running a partner model close to an auction is useful infrastructure, but it does not by itself solve:

- which specialist should be invoked;
- how contradictory or correlated evidence is handled;
- how causal contribution is measured;
- how specialist competence is calibrated over time; or
- whether the learning remains portable rather than becoming permanent property of one coordinator.

Those are the higher-order research questions.

### Kill test

Reject or narrow the architecture if:

- bilateral integrations scale adequately;
- proprietary coordinators solve the problem without meaningful lock-in or lost value;
- specialist intelligence cannot demonstrate incremental causal contribution;
- latency, governance, privacy, or semantic coordination costs erase the benefit; or
- portable competence creates more complexity than value.

### Long-horizon branch

If bounded OpenDecisioning works, a later **Open Decision Network / Open Decision Exchange** could explore competence routing, causal calibration, coordinator portability, and market mechanisms for independently owned intelligence.

That future architecture is not required for the first product proof.

### Public boundary

This is R&D only. It is not a claim that a particular publisher, DSP, SSP, or standards body has adopted the architecture, and it is not a claim of patent novelty.

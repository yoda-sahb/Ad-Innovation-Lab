# Agentic Advertising Operating System

## Autonomous execution is easier than autonomous accountability

**Status:** `CONCEPT`  
**Type:** governed multi-party decision and commitment layer

### Possibility

Advertising agents can increasingly plan, negotiate, activate, optimize, and transact.

The limiting system question is not whether an agent can call an API.

It is whether the market can represent, enforce, and audit:

- who the agent represents;
- what objective it is authorized to pursue;
- what money, data, inventory, tools, and actions it may use;
- what constraints remain non-negotiable;
- who owns conflicting decisions across buyer and seller boundaries;
- what commitments the agent has made; and
- how a bad action is explained, disputed, suspended, or rolled back.

### Mechanism

A model-neutral control layer would sit above existing execution systems rather than replacing them.

Candidate primitives include:

- signed delegation;
- capability / permission registry;
- decision contract;
- policy engine;
- governed context and provenance;
- immutable decision ledger;
- evidence-quality labels;
- approval / exception paths; and
- versioned model or policy promotion rather than uncontrolled self-modification.

Existing DSPs, SSPs, ad servers, and market APIs remain the execution rails.

### Authority matters

A useful system must preserve distinct decision rights.

Examples:

- buyer owns budget, objective, and brand risk;
- publisher owns inventory, pricing, eligibility, and customer experience;
- platform owns marketplace integrity and reliability;
- data owner controls permitted data use;
- accountable human operators retain policy and exception responsibility.

The architecture fails if “agentic” becomes a reason to blur those boundaries.

### First proof

Define a minimum portable decision contract for a small number of real multi-party actions, such as:

- autonomous premium-video deal activation;
- publisher-side model execution; or
- governed cross-platform budget reallocation.

Test whether multiple participants identify a real reduction in integration or governance burden.

### Kill test

Reject or narrow the thesis if:

- bilateral APIs and platform-specific governance are sufficient;
- portable authority semantics create more latency or ambiguity than they remove;
- participants will not expose enough authority metadata to make the contract useful;
- human approval remains necessary for most material actions; or
- the mechanism duplicates existing standards without a measurable operating advantage.

### What this is not

- a chatbot for media buying;
- an agent marketplace;
- a replacement DSP or SSP;
- a permissionless autonomous trading system; or
- a claim that self-modifying agents should control live financial decisions.

### Public boundary

This is a research hypothesis about governance and system architecture. It is not a deployed product claim or a representation of any company’s internal agent architecture.

# Experience Exchange

## What if the impression becomes too small for the intelligence making the decision?

**Status:** `WORKING NORTH STAR · DO NOT BUILD YET`  
**Type:** future demand-architecture research branch

### Possibility

Advertising intelligence is beginning to reason across much richer state than a sequence of isolated impression auctions naturally represents.

That state can include intent, commerce behavior, prior exposure, creative response, customer state, channel, time, measurement, expected outcome, suppression, and non-impression actions.

The long-horizon question is not merely whether bidding gets smarter.

It is whether the **impression itself eventually becomes too narrow as the universal optimization and transaction unit.**

### Mechanism

The working abstraction is:

`advertiser mandate + current experience state + eligible action set + constraints + intelligence → marginal action values → allocate / abstain → execute → bind outcome → update state`

In this architecture, an impression remains useful, but it becomes one possible execution mechanism rather than the thing the economic system must optimize by definition.

The term **exchange** is earned only if multiple eligible actions, surfaces, suppliers, or execution paths are actually compared or cleared under shared economic state.

If the system is simply a smarter bidder or orchestrator, it is not an Experience Exchange.

### The railroad test

> **Intelligence is the train. The bidding and transaction system is the track. First prove the train is constrained by the track before rebuilding the railroad.**

Experience Bidding is the bridge experiment.

First prove that longitudinal state and marginal-next-action valuation matter on existing rails. Only then test whether the rails themselves prevent further value.

### What the current rail might constrain

Research should test whether impression-centric execution materially limits:

- the action space intelligence can choose from;
- continuity of advertiser/customer state across decisions;
- coordination across surface, channel, creative, offer, and time;
- feedback speed from outcomes into the next economic decision;
- abstention, suppression, or non-impression actions;
- causal learning across successive actions; and
- coherent comparison of heterogeneous next actions under one advertiser mandate.

### Proof

Before designing any new exchange, compare at least three bounded systems using the same advertiser objective and outcome evidence:

1. ordinary impression bidding;
2. Experience Bidding on existing rails;
3. an action-set selector that can compare multiple eligible next actions.

The question is not whether #3 looks more sophisticated.

The question is whether the existing rail creates a measurable ceiling on achievable advertiser value.

### Kill test

Reject or narrow the thesis if:

- modern DSPs can absorb richer state and intelligence while continuing to transact impression-by-impression with no material loss of value;
- Experience Bidding captures most of the available gain without changing transaction semantics;
- heterogeneous actions cannot be compared coherently under advertiser objectives;
- coordination, privacy, rights, latency, governance, or publisher-economics costs exceed the value created; or
- a simpler orchestration/control-plane design produces equivalent results.

### Relationship to OpenDecisioning

Experience Exchange asks **what demand is valuing and allocating.**

OpenDecisioning and the Open Decision Exchange line ask **which independently owned intelligence contributes to a decision, how contribution is evaluated, and how competence remains portable.**

The ideas may eventually interoperate. They are not the same architecture.

### Naming boundary

“Experience Exchange” is a provisional research label, not a final product name, category claim, or claim of novelty.
